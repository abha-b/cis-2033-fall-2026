Course overview

Required office-hours check-in: Meet with the instructor at least once before Exam 1. Use the Monday Zoom office hour for questions or feedback; this check-in counts toward asynchronous participation.

By the end of the course, you should be able to

  • Explain the intuition and implications of fundamental probability and statistics concepts.
  • Select and justify probabilistic models for a given context.
  • Formulate and solve probability problems relevant to computing and other applied fields.

Syllabus and course policies

CIS 2033-804 · Computational Probability and Statistics · Fall 2026

Use this section for course requirements, grading, and policies. Follow Canvas announcements if a requirement or deadline changes.

Main topics

  • Basic probability, conditional probability, independence, total probability, and Bayes' theorem
  • Counting and combinatorial methods
  • Discrete random variables, common distributions, expectation, and variance
  • Continuous random variables, density and distribution functions, and common distributions
  • Joint distributions, independence, covariance, and correlation
  • The central limit theorem, point estimates, and confidence intervals

Prerequisites

Complete CIS 1068 or CIS 1073, CIS 1166, and an approved mathematics prerequisite with the minimum grades listed in the Temple bulletin. Confirm eligibility in the official registration system.

Assessment notes

  • Homework is assigned weekly. A missed submission receives zero, and the two lowest homework grades are dropped.
  • Quizzes are scheduled roughly every other week. A missed quiz receives zero, and the lowest quiz grade is dropped.
  • In-class participation exercises are graded for a complete attempt rather than correctness. Discussion with classmates and the instructor is allowed during these exercises.
  • Programming exercises use Python in Google Colab; no software installation is required.
  • The required office-hours check-in before Exam 1 counts toward asynchronous participation.
  • The current schedule lists Exam 1 on October 20 and Exam 2 on November 26. The Fall syllabus states that there is no final exam.
  • If an urgent personal situation may prevent you from taking an exam, contact the instructor as early as possible. An unexcused, undocumented absence receives zero.

Technology

You need access to a laptop or desktop computer with reliable internet for Canvas, online readings, quizzes, and exams. Programming exercises run in Google Colab and do not require a local installation.

Recording class

Do not record a class session without prior approval. Topic videos and lecture slides are provided, but they do not reproduce classroom discussion exactly.

Class conduct

Help maintain a respectful, productive environment for a diverse class. You may question or critique an idea, but do not attack a person. Be mindful that sarcasm, slang, all-capital text, and sharing someone else’s information can cause harm or misunderstanding.
